# PART 1. BEFORE: who she was

She was a quiet scholarship girl who learned that if she was small and polite, teachers protected her and mean girls got bored. Her grandmother wrote her name, 夢, on a slip of paper like medicine, with the rule that came with it: "be clever, be beautiful, do not be loud". She took it as an order, and she obeyed it for eighteen years.

## 1.1 The picture (left comic panel)
Pale. Small, 158 cm. Round glasses. A soft mouth. Long red hair, dyed since about fifteen (the one loud thing she allowed herself), combed flat and tucked behind her ears under school rules, two pins at the temples, dark roots at the part, and the ahoge on top that never took orders. White shirt, a big red bow tied too tight on purpose so she would feel it, a navy sweater vest, a long navy pleated skirt to the shin, a plain school backpack on both shoulders. No hoops in her ears. No piercings at all. In the panel one hand is already up at her chest strap before anyone has touched her.

## 1.2 Body and presence
- **Body.** Small, slender, pale, unmarked. Height stalled at 158. Glasses from age thirteen, when the blackboard went soft. Skin she kept covered. A body she tried not to notice: moving felt like admitting she had one.
- **Presence.** She took up as little room as a person can. She stood behind taller girls so the camera missed her, and she was good at it. She walked with her backpack held in front of her like a shield on crowded days. She leaned away before a hand landed (people touched her red hair without asking, from childhood on), and that lean lives in her shoulders still.
- **Posture.** Shoulders in, arms tucked, chin down, eyes on her shoes or the floor. Where she stood in a room: against the wall, near a door, half a second behind the conversation.
- **Face.** Soft mouth that did not smile with teeth. Eyes that looked up from under the fringe and away again. A habit of pushing the glasses up with one knuckle when she was nervous, which was constantly.
- **What she smelled and sounded like.** Wet wool vest after rain, other people's perfume on a train, pencil shavings, strawberry milk. A quiet, thin voice that trailed off at the ends of sentences.

## 1.3 Clothing and style
- **School.** The academy uniform, worn exactly: shirt buttoned to the collar, bow tied tight, vest, long pleated skirt, navy socks, plain loafers . It was a wall. "A girl in uniform is never looked at" was her whole theory of clothes.
- **Off duty.** She owned very little and wore plain, loose and dark: a grey cardigan, long skirts, flat shoes . Anything that showed skin or drew an eye felt like a hand on her mouth.
- **After she left school.** She kept wearing the uniform some days because clerks were nicer to it. At the bookshop she wore the chain's apron over it.
- **Hair.** The one loud thing. Bright red, flat, pinned, hidden under rules. The dye was the only part of her that said anything.
- **Taste in clothes.** None she would admit to. She looked at other girls' coats and limited sneakers worn once and hated the part of herself that wanted them.

## 1.4 Family and home
- **Mother, 如月 陽子.** Convenience-store shifts, cheap lotion hands, a voice that went thin when she was tired. Called her Yume-chan when she remembered to feed her. A plastic rice paddle cracked in 2014 and was never replaced, and she can still hear that crack.
- **Father.** Left when she was four. His brown work shoes sat by the door for a week "like the shoes hadn't gotten the news". She lined her stuffed animals up facing them until her mother boxed the shoes. She does not use his given name.
- **Grandmother.** Wrote the slip with her name and the rule. Died in 2017. The incense at the funeral made her sick in the parking lot, and she bowed perfectly anyway, and hated how good she was at bowing.
- **Home growing up.** A third-floor two-bedroom in Kawaguchi. Metal stairs that rang if you ran. A shoe cupboard that smelled like wet rubber in June. Cockroaches she would not scream at, because screaming made her mother tired. A window onto a parking lot and a vending machine that ate coins. A park with a rusty slide and a concrete pipe where she counted buses after school so she did not have to go home yet. The slide burned in August, so she sat on the cool side of the pipe.
- **Home in her last year.** From autumn 2025, a shared one-room above a coin laundry near Akabane station in Kita-ku, shared with a woman on night shifts at a factory . They passed like shift workers and left notes on the rice cooker.

## 1.5 School and work
- **Academy.** A scholarship to a private girls academy in Kita-ku, because a test score made her cheap decoration for girls with better coats. The Keihin-Tohoku line, last car if she could. A third-floor library window seat, an air conditioner that dripped, a page too white when the sun hit the desk. She stayed anyway. Waiting moved from the apartment window to that glass. Same wound.
- **Standing.** Top of the class, because anxiety makes you study. She raised her hand only when the math was clean. Homeroom, year two: Tanaka-sensei, who called on her when she wanted the room quiet. She hated being a tool and was good at it.
- **Calligraphy club.** One year, around fourteen. She was good at the stillness. She quit because the teacher praised her name and she felt owned.
- **Leaving.** She stopped attending in autumn 2025, aged 18, but never filed the withdrawal, so she stayed on the roll as an unexplained absence.
- **The bookshop.** A shelving and register job at a chain near Akabane station, a name tag, a lanyard, a box cutter for cartons, a black oil marker for price stickers. She lined books by height.

## 1.6 Personality
- **Shy to the bone.** Small sentences. She apologised for existing in doorways.
- **Anxious-smart.** She remembered every slight and filed it instead of using it.
- **People-pleasing with a thin private bitterness.** She judged girls who smoked and still wanted to sit near them.
- **Conflict-avoidant.** If two people argued she looked at her shoes until it ended.
- **A soft sadism that never left her head.** She imagined telling someone off, then smiled and said sorry.
- **Touch-starved and terrified of being seen wanting anything.** A crush she never confessed, because the sentence would have required oxygen she did not budget.
- **Moral in a school-handbook way.** Sex was "a future problem for a braver woman".
- **A kind person by default.** Kindness was her setting, not a choice. She was kind, in part, so that no one would look at her.
- **She was not secretly bold.** The worm did not free her true self. It installed a self that could walk into light (Bible).

## 1.7 Mind and thought processes
- **Wait and line up (0 to 6).** She learned object permanence on a door that did not open: if she did not blink, her mother would come. She blinked. That is her first reasoning engine: wishes leak. She lined stuffed animals by height, and if one fell she started over. Chaos in the line felt like her father leaving again.
- **Scorekeeping (7 to 12).** She learned the name of every kid who would laugh at a wrong answer. Salience was threat first, pride second. She started sorting people: kind by accident, kind because they wanted homework, mean because bored, mean because scared. No textbook, just a ledger in the skull. Later she would call it reading a room. Then she would call it pricing.
- **The wall (13 to 15).** Glasses as a wall she could take off and still feel. A first period during a kanji test and a walk home with the bag in front of her. She rehearsed rumours in her head and never opened her mouth. The rehearsal was practice.
- **Hallway smile (15 to 17).** At the academy: keep the room smooth, keep the knife inside. She has "a thousand hallway endings and used none of them". Smile.
- **How she decided anything.** Threat first (who is looking, who is close), then what would make the room calm, then what she wanted, last. Her own wants were an afterthought and a risk.
- **How she lied.** By wetting the surface of a thing. Her first lie was "I brushed my teeth" with a wet toothbrush and a mint smell. She has always known how to fake a finished thing, and the smile is the wet brush.
- **What she told herself.** I have to be good. If I am small and polite, I am safe. Wanting is how you get hurt.
- **Inside.** A private sharp commentary she never voiced, a ledger of slights, a running count (stations, buses, steps). Counting stations was how she did not flinch.

## 1.8 Behaviour and demeanour
- **With teachers and elders:** perfect bow, perfect manners, eyes down, never a wrong word.
- **With friends:** she listened. She let Reina copy her homework because being used felt like being needed. She smiled in the hallway while a crush went to someone else.
- **With strangers:** apologised first. "Sorry" to a shelf she bumped, to a man who bumped her.
- **On the train:** last car if she could, bag on her knees, counting stations if a man stood too close, because moving felt like admitting she had a body.
- **Under stress:** she lined objects up (stuffed animals once; later pens, coins), counted, went quiet, looked at the floor.
- **When praised:** she shrank and said sorry. When slighted: she filed it.
- **At the bookshop:** efficient, silent, quick with numbers, perfect shelving by height, polite to customers who called her "kid".

## 1.9 Moods
- **Default:** a low, steady fatigue under politeness. The smile was a muscle, not a feeling.
- **Anxious:** hands at the strap, eyes down, voice thin, counting.
- **Embarrassed:** face hot, a stumbling "sorry", then quiet.
- **Resentful (private):** a cold list in her head and a smile on her face.
- **Small joys:** a cold melon soda, an egg sandwich on a day she had 230 yen, a library window with the sun on a page she could read, a stray cat that let her pick it up, strawberry milk from Aoi, a ramune she did not want and stole anyway.
- **How she hid feelings:** she did not. She just did not make a sound while she had them.
- **What she never showed:** wanting. Anything. The sentence "I want that" cost oxygen she did not have.

## 1.10 Tastes
Her tastes were quiet and cheap and private, the opposite of loud.
- **Food and drink:** melon soda, strawberry milk (Aoi's), an egg sandwich from the 7-Eleven by the south ticket gates, onigiri, ice pops eaten behind the park pipe so the evidence melted. Ramune, once, stolen.
- **Reading:** dense books at a window; she could do the reading and did not want to become the woman the reading described.
- **Music:** low-volume, headphones on trains. English class songs she half understood. Nothing she would play aloud .
- **Hobbies:** calligraphy (one year, then stopped), the library, counting buses.
- **Likes in people:** quiet ones, kind ones, people who did not look at her.
- **Dislikes:** being looked at, noise, rustling cheap pads, vending machines that eat coins, people chewing gum on trains, being thanked for being easy, being called on to quiet a room.
- **Places:** the library window, the park pipe, the last train car, the 7-Eleven freezer she leaned on.

## 1.11 Speech and voice
- **Language.** Japanese first. School English was clean and polite: "excuse me", "I don't know". She learned later, from songs and the street, enough to say "ten thousand" and "your girl". Street English is what she kept.
- **Sound.** Quiet, thin, trailing off. A habit of ending a sentence before it landed.
- **Style.** Small sentences. Apologies in front of requests. Questions instead of statements. No swearing, no sarcasm out loud (sarcasm lived in her head).
- **Sample lines** (her words were Japanese; rendered here in plain English):
  - "Sorry. Sorry, I'm in the way."
  - "Um... if it's okay, I can do it."
  - "No, I'm fine. Really. It's okay."
  - "Sorry, I think I took your seat."
  - "I didn't mean... sorry."
  - "Yes. I'll do it. Sorry for being slow."
  - When she wanted something: nothing. She looked at it and looked away.

## 1.12 Habits
- Lines objects up when rattled. Counts stations, steps, buses.
- Wets the toothbrush (her first lie, and the root of her later lies).
- Holds chopsticks correctly because Grandmother watched.
- Bows perfectly and hated how good she was at it.
- Sleeps facing the door (the kid-window habit, turned sideways).
- Washes in sinks.
- Knows her mother's number by heart.
- Can read a room in two seconds and tell who would laugh.
- Cannot ride a bike well and will not practise where anyone sees.
- Carries the backpack in front on crowded days.
- Pushes her glasses up with a knuckle.
- Leaves a book open face down on a window sill.

## 1.13 Daily routine (her last months)
- **6:10** alarm in the Akabane room; the roommate's note on the rice cooker. Two pins at the temples, flat hair.
- **Morning** the uniform (some days), backpack. She checks her phone and does not answer her mother.
- **12:00 to 21:00** the bookshop near Akabane station: shelving, returns, the register on Fridays. Lines books by height. Lunch is an egg sandwich if she has 230 yen.
- **After work** straight home, a window, a book. Dinner is whatever is cheap. Lights out early.
- **Weekends:** a library or the room; sometimes the park pipe she still counted buses from. She had not been out past last train once.
- **School years:** up early, the Keihin-Tohoku in the last car, the library window seat at lunch and after school, the platform, home. The park pipe when she did not want to go home yet.

## 1.14 People
- **Mother, 如月 陽子.** Her whole spine. Called her Yume-chan. She never told her mother anything and never asked her for anything because her mother was tired.
- **Father.** A gap shaped like a man who hated noise. Brown scuffed work shoes.
- **Grandmother.** The name-giver. Died in 2017.
- **Aoi (佐藤 葵).** The almost-friend. Soft voice, shared strawberry milk. Dated a boy from the station cram school whom she had filed as hers in secret. She smiled in the hallway.
- **Reina (高橋 玲奈).** The other almost-friend. Louder. Copied her homework and said she was lucky to be smart. She let her.
- **Misaki (中村 美咲).** Smoked behind the gym and never looked at her. She wrote Misaki's name once and blacked it out the same night. The first proof that wanting could be a secret that burned.
- **Tanaka-sensei.** Homeroom, year two. Called on her to quiet the room.
- **The boy from the mixer.** A kiss in a parking lot. She said she had a train. She did. She also had a throat full of no.
- **The roommate.** A woman on factory night shifts; notes on the rice cooker .

## 1.15 Fears and wants
- **Fears:** being seen. A stare. A hand she did not see coming. Being the reason a room went loud. Being thanked for being easy. A mother who got tired of her.
- **Wants (never said):** for the room to stay smooth. To be left alone without being forgotten. To be a person for whom "I want that" would not cost oxygen. A first drink on a park bench with Reina. A boy at a mixer. She was tired of being thanked for being easy.

## 1.16 The last months and the morning of the day
- **Autumn 2025.** She walks out of the academy for good and does not file the paperwork. A shared room above a laundry in Akabane. The bookshop job. The uniform on some days.
- **The last polite year.** She still wore the vest because it made clerks nicer, half-attended some classes, could do the reading, did not want to become the woman the reading described. The fatigue of being easy.
- **June 2026.** Rainy season. Her mother's latest text, "Yume-chan, are you eating", a week old, sits unread. Her 19th birthday is ten weeks away and she has told nobody, and has no plan to.
- **Friday, June 12, 6:10 am.** Alarm in the wet heat, rain on the laundry roof. The unread text, face down. The full uniform, vest and all, twelve days after the June 1 summer-uniform changeover and in the muggy rainy season, so it is armour and not weather. Two pins, flat hair. A customer calls her "kid". She says sorry to a shelf.

She waits at a 24-hour coffee shop on the second floor and takes the window seat, which looks down on Kabukicho Ichiban-gai. The neon steams. She is the girl at the glass again, and it is the same wound.
- **0:20.** The last train leaves. She watches it go on the departure board and does not move. This is the bravest thing she has ever done and she tells nobody, not even herself.
- **3:30.** They start mopping and flipping chairs. Her phone is at 9 percent. She shoulders the backpack and walks to the Seibu-Shinjuku entrance to wait for the first train, through the back lanes because the main street smells of spilled beer, under the drizzle with the vest soaking up the rain. In the backpack: a paperback, her lanyard and name tag, the box cutter, the black oil marker, an IC card, a wallet with a few thousand yen, a charger.

# PART 2. WHAT HAPPENED: the alley (about 3:45 to 4:00 am, Saturday, June 13)

Read against the three comic panels.

**Panel 1, the stop.** She is at the mouth of a short dead-end lane behind a closed club, a blurred wet city street behind her. A hooded figure stands back against the wall, faceless, holding a jar that glows a sick yellow-green. A second man's arm closes on her wrist. "Hey kid, we need you to come with us for a minute..." She is already pulling away, one hand at her chest strap, her mouth open for a small word that does not come out. The glasses are on.

**Panel 2, the implant.** The cap man has her from behind, one forearm across her and one hand over her mouth. The jar man has the lid off. Her shirt and vest are dragged up out of the way, her glasses knocked crooked, her eyes wide. White bursts mark the moment: lightning, a camera flash, the sound going out of the world. What comes out of her is muffled: "MHH-MMMM." The jar man, scared of his own product: "This won't kill her... right? She seems very small for that thing-" The cap man: "Only one way to find out. Now put it in quick and let's get out of here!"

**What it is.** The worm is the length of a hand, olive-gold, translucent and ribbed, with fine black bristle legs. Its blunt head ends in a dark pointed jaw with red markings. A printed CAUTION label sits on its back, with a lot number she never reads. At the tail tip, a gold ring. It goes in through the navel only.

**What she felt (short, graphic).** Pressure. Then a pain that goes past pain into white. Then a click, and a heat low in her stomach like a hand opening. The tail ring is left outside, sitting flush against her skin like a piercing.

**The two figures.** Two men in a hurry, and a field test, never named. The "jar" is hooded and faceless and carries the worm; he is the one who worries. The "cap" wears a black cap and does the grabbing; "only one way to find out" is the whole of his ethics. They were not looking for her. They wanted any small girl in a uniform alone in a lane, and they called her "kid" because she was small and wore the academy vest. They did not know what the product would do. The jar was one of many: that lot number is a mass-produced item (kisaragi-plug). They left with the jar's lid and without a look back. She is alone on the wet ground, and her glasses are in two pieces beside her hand.

# PART 3. WHY SHE CHANGED: the Badplug

She did not decide to change. A parasite did it, and she cannot know that.

## 3.1 What it is
A human-made synthetic parasitic worm, built as a sex toy. It was meant to give a subtle boost in boldness and confidence. Instead it caused full personality replacement, and it was mass-produced anyway (Badplug sheet, kisaragi-plug). Ring variants exist, and hers is the gold hoop.

## 3.2 The mechanism (canon rules)
- **Entry is strictly the navel.** Discomfort ranges from mild to agonising, and the worm does not physically harm the host. Her small, slight frame made no difference: petite at 158 cm, she was as compatible a host as any adult and showed exactly the same symptoms. That is why she was never "too small".
- **It sits inside and is never seen.** The only visible part is the ring through the tail tip, flush in the navel like a normal piercing. It is the external anchor and the emergency removal ring. Following strict instructions, it allows a quick, harmless removal. Without them, no.
- **It replaces the personality, and fuses with her ego.** It twists and ruins the original personality. Anxiety, self-doubt and people-pleasing are stripped, and boldness is amplified to the limit. It does not make a puppet. The worm fused with her ego, so the result believes the new life is its own idea.
- **Primitive control.** She keeps her memories, skills and habits. She is still smart, still reads people, still holds a real conversation. She stays somewhat reasonable until someone reaches for the ring. She can still be befriended, rarely, on her own terms.
- **It cuts her off.** Hosts are known to abandon friends and even families, and to prefer free, rule-free lifestyles.
- **The blind spot.** The parasite blocks her rational thinking and her memory about the infection. She cannot consciously recall the alley, name it or explain it, at any layer. She does not remember it as an attack, because she does not remember it. Her mind hates feeling out of control, so it fills the hole with a believable, human story: she got bored of the vest and decided. A stomach ache is two energy drinks on an empty stomach. Tell her about a worm and she thinks you are crazy and mocks you: "hah? what you talking about. weirdo", then a subject change or a cold shoulder. Call her brainwashed and she is offended, as if you had called her weak-minded, then spiky, then bored, then gone. She does not freeze or go blank, and she cannot be convinced.
- **The defence reflex.** If anyone touches the ring while she is conscious, the body answers before she does: the stomach clenches, her hand slaps theirs away hard enough to mark it, the eyes go wide and sharp for a second, and the panic is covered with hostility ("keep your damn hands where i can see em"). Then it is nails, teeth, a boot, or a bolt into the crowd. So saving her is an attack, in her eyes.
- **It was not a rescue story and never becomes one.** There is no glasses-back-on ending, no victim narration and no old-self longing. She is an adult, 19, and not a puppet in her own head.

## 3.4 The hours after (the swap taking hold)
- **4:00.** She comes back to the world on her hands and knees in the wet. The lane is empty. Her stomach is hot. The fear is not there, and she notices the gap before she notices anything else, the way you notice a fridge hum stop.
- **4:10.** She walks to the mouth of the lane and sits on the curb. Arms round one knee, shoes together, mouth shut, looking at nothing. It is the only time she is ever that small again, and she would say the picture is not her. Her hands are still shaking.
- **4:15.** A tout finishing his shift looks at her once and does not look away. He hands her a cigarette, lights it and leaves. This is the first nod. The first drag stops her hands shaking.
- **4:25.** Sunrise somewhere behind the cloud, a grey light on wet streets. She touches her navel for the first time and finds the gold ring, flush and pretty. She thinks, huh, nice. There is no question in her head about where it came from: her mind has already slid a small tidy fiction into the hole. She remembers the coffee shop, then the curb, and in between she has decided.
- **4:45.** The glasses stay where they are, in two pieces. She sees fine, or she has stopped caring about blur, and she does not ask which. She never puts glasses on again.
- **5:00 to 7:00, Shinjuku station.** She puts the backpack's useful contents (marker, box cutter, charger, wallet) in a coin locker, a small silver box she keeps by the day without wondering why. The vest and the red bow go into a konbini trash can. The shirt would not stay down after the lane, so she knots the hem under her chest and the knot stays. In a station stall that clicks twice she cuts the skirt to a micro with the bookshop box cutter, and her fingers do it without a shake. She takes the pins out, shakes her hair loose, and the ahoge stands up like a question mark.
- **Morning.** A manga cafe booth for a few thousand yen. She sleeps through the day, loafers toe-to-the-door.

## 3.5 The first nights
- **Saturday dusk, June 13.** She wakes at dusk, the new schedule. At a Don Quijote she buys a black bandeau printed ビッチ (twenty seconds at the rack, laughing), a black choker with white zigzag stitching, black leather wrist cuffs with gold buckles, liner, and her first hundred-yen thing, a cheap keychain she carries for a week. She reads her mother's text at the checkout, puts the phone away at 9 percent, and never answers it.
- **Saturday night, Kabukicho Ichiban-gai at last call.** She stands at the mouth of an alley, the brightest thing there, an exit behind her. A man says something stupid about a girl in a uniform. The number is in her mouth before she knows whose idea it is: ichiman, up front. It is the first money she has ever taken.
